Paying for reasoning, and where it is wasted

What you are buying

The model writes reasoning tokens before its answer. Those bill as output, which is the expensive kind.

considers, checks, discards"] TH --> A["Answer"] TH -.->|"billed as output
never cached"| C["💰"]

A 4,000-token reasoning block costs more than the entire input prompt on most calls. So the only question worth asking is where it pays.

On the right-hand tasks the reasoning block restates the rubric, then applies it. That is what it would have done anyway.

Budget by volume, not by importance

Same logic as model routing: cost of being wrong, divided by how often the call happens.

per run?"} V -->|"1–2"| W{"Wrong answer
expensive?"} V -->|"tens"| NO["No budget
reasoning would dominate cost"] W -->|yes| YES["Generous budget"] W -->|no| SM["Small or none"]
Role Calls Budget Why
Planner 1-2 per run Generous A wrong order wastes solver-hours
Proposal 1 per diagnosis Moderate Must weigh evidence and justify itself
Tool choice up to 4 None A four-way pick with a clear rubric
Grader up to 32 None Highest volume; the rubric already decides

The grader is the clearest case. Adding 500 reasoning tokens to 32 calls multiplies the system's biggest cost line for a judgement the rubric already determines.

What it interacts with

Two of these surprise people: no cache discount, and latency multiplied by loop iterations.
Interacts with Consequence
Prompt caching Caching covers input. Thinking is output, so never discounted.
Structured outputs Reasoning comes first. Parse the final block, never the reasoning.
Latency Seconds of generation, multiplied by loop iterations.
Durable replay No change. Still a non-deterministic activity.
Observability Record reasoning tokens separately, or a cost jump looks like traffic.
Injection defence Unchanged. The closed output enum is still the backstop.

Reasoning budget inside a bounded loop

Symptom: enabling thinking pushes diagnosis past its deadline and the activity times out.

Cause: the budget applies per call, so four iterations multiply it.

Mitigation: put the budget on the single proposal call at the end. One considered decision over collected evidence beats four deliberations over partial evidence.

Find the knee

Treat the budget as a parameter you sweep, not a setting you pick once.
flowchart LR S1["Pick the metric
that role owns"] --> S2["Sweep the budget
across a few values"] S2 --> S3["Plot metric
vs cost per call"] S3 --> S4["Take the knee"] S4 -->|"model version changes"| S1

Returns flatten. Going from none to a moderate budget usually gives most of the gain. Doubling again gives much less for twice the output bill, and where that knee sits depends on your task.

A reasoning block is the model's working, not an audit trail. It can describe a path that differs from how the answer was produced. The auditable record is the typed output plus its validated evidence IDs.

Takeaways

  • Thinking tokens bill as output and are never cached, so volume calls are where they hurt.
  • Spend on low-volume, high-stakes reasoning: planning and final proposals.
  • Skip it on lookups, rubric classification, and extraction.
  • Inside a loop, budget once at the end rather than per iteration.
  • Sweep against a metric and take the knee. Re-run when the model changes.
